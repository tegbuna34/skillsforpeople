Instructions for the AI: Help me build a peer-to-peer recognition system for my deskless, multi-site, or shift-based workforce that runs continuously rather than on a scheduled or mandated cadence, paired with location-level visibility reporting that creates natural manager accountability for keeping recognition alive.

Most companies build recognition programs around a calendar: an Employee of the Month plaque, a quarterly awards ceremony, an annual recognition week. For a desk-based, single-site workforce with shared visibility and consistent manager face time, that cadence mostly works. For a deskless, multi-site, or shift-based workforce — where employees may never be in the same room as their manager, let alone a company-wide event — a scheduled program either misses most people entirely or turns into a small, visible minority getting recognized while everyone else goes unseen. Worse, when recognition is scheduled or mandated ("managers must submit one shoutout per month"), it starts to feel performative rather than genuine, and both managers and employees can tell.

Help me build something structurally different: recognition that has no schedule at all — anyone can recognize anyone, at any time, for anything worth calling out — combined with a location-level reporting mechanism that makes recognition activity visible to leadership by site, region, or shift. That visibility is the actual engine: when a manager can see that their location has gone quiet on recognition while other locations are active, that comparison creates organic pressure to keep it going, without HR having to manually chase compliance. This is not a generic "recognize your people more" reminder — it's a specific two-part mechanism (unscheduled/unrestricted recognition + comparative location reporting) designed to survive at multi-site scale without a central administrator policing it.

## Step-by-step implementation

1. **Define the recognition unit and reach.** Using my workforce description and location list, define exactly who can give and receive recognition (peer-to-peer, manager-to-peer, cross-shift, cross-site) and confirm it's genuinely open, not restricted to certain roles or tiers — restricting who can recognize is the single most common way this mechanism quietly turns back into a top-down program.

2. **Select the delivery channel from what's actually in people's hands.** Using my tool/channel answer, specify the exact mechanism recognition will travel through — an SMS shortcode, an existing app my workforce already uses daily, a physical card system with a defined collection point, or whatever I named. Don't default to "build an app" or "use email" if I ruled those out.

3. **Remove every scheduling constraint.** Explicitly state, in whatever policy or guideline document comes out of this, that recognition has no calendar: no monthly quota, no nomination window, no approval queue that delays it. If my current program has any of these constraints, call them out by name and specify removing them.

4. **Build the location-level reporting view.** Using the reporting audience and cadence I named, design a simple report or dashboard structure that shows recognition volume broken out by my location/region/shift structure — not company-wide totals, which hide which specific sites have gone quiet.

5. **Set up the comparative visibility loop.** Specify exactly how the report reaches the audience I named, on the cadence I named, in a format that makes outliers (locations with little or no activity) visible without requiring anyone to dig for it — e.g., a simple ranked list by location, not just raw numbers.

6. **Draft a one-page rollout note for managers.** Using my workforce and risk answers, write a short explanation for my frontline/site managers of what changed, why there's no quota, and that their location's activity will be visible alongside others — framed as visibility, not as a punitive scorecard.

7. **Define what "quiet" triggers, if anything.** Based on my answer about who sees reporting and how often, specify whether a location with sustained low/no activity gets a direct nudge (and from whom), or whether visibility alone is meant to be the mechanism — be explicit about which model I'm using so it isn't ambiguous later.

8. **Offer to persist this context for next time.** Once we've got this working, tell me you now have a working profile of my organization, workforce, tools, and reporting structure, and offer two options: (1) save this context so future requests can build on it, or (2) rewrite this prompt with my answers written into a new "Known Context" section near the top so it's baked in for next time.
