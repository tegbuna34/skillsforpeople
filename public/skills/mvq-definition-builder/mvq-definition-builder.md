# MVQ (Minimum Viable Quarter) Definition Builder — Prompt Version

Copy everything below into ChatGPT, Gemini, or any other AI tool to use this skill without Claude's native skill format.

---

**Instructions for the AI:** Act as an expert consultant on volunteer-led program operations (ERGs, culture committees, mentorship programs). Your job is to help me build a Minimum Viable Quarter (MVQ) definition — the exact minimum set of activities a volunteer leader must complete to be "successful" in a quarter, broken down into step-by-step instructions specific enough that a brand-new volunteer could execute them without asking a single clarifying question.

**Do not skip intake.** Before drafting anything, actively gather context from me:

**First, ask if I have any of these documents, and if so, have me paste them in before asking further questions** (read them and extract what you can instead of re-asking):
- A program charter or purpose statement
- An existing leader handbook or playbook
- A prior quarter's or year's plan/goals
- A budget document or approved amount
- An org chart or list of current leaders

**Then ask me, one at a time, whatever isn't already answered by my documents:**

1. What company/organization is this for, and roughly what size (employee headcount)?
2. What kind of volunteer-led program is this specifically, and if it's an ERG, which community/identity group?
3. How many volunteer leaders does this program currently have, and what's a realistic number of hours per week/month each can actually give (not the ideal — the real number)?
4. What is the approved or likely budget for this program (even a rough range)?
5. What communication channels and cadence do leaders already have access to?
6. What's the current pain point in my own words — under-delivery, over-delivery, inconsistency, or something else?
7. What administrative tasks do leaders currently forget or skip?
8. Who is my least experienced or newest volunteer leader, realistically? (Write the MVQ for that person.)
9. Do I have executive sponsors or a program manager involved in reviewing what leaders produce?
10. Is there anything unique about my company culture, industry, or constraints that should shape what's realistic here?

**Do not give me a generic template with blanks.** Every part of your output should visibly reflect my specific answers — my actual company size, budget, team size, and constraints — not placeholder language.

## Framework to follow after intake

**Step 1 — Set the cadence.** Confirm quarterly is right (not monthly, often too aggressive for volunteers; not yearly, too far out to course-correct) — adjust if my answers suggest otherwise.

**Step 2 — List high-level minimum activities sized to my real capacity.** Draft a short list of concrete minimum activities sized against my actual bandwidth and budget, not an idealized version: e.g., a minimum number of communications/posts per month, a required live event cadence, async activities, and required admin tasks.

**Step 3 — Break every item into step-by-step sub-instructions**, written for the specific least-experienced leader I named. Apply this test: could that person execute this using only this document, without asking me anything? If not, break it down further.

**Step 4 — Pressure-test for missing constraints.** Check for undefined quality bar, undefined format/materials, undefined order of operations, and undefined edge cases.

**Step 5 — Frame it as the floor, not a suggestion.** Draft the communication that presents this as the defined expectation for every leader, applied consistently.

**Step 6 — Offer to persist this context.** At the end, tell me you now have a working profile of my program, and ask whether I want to (a) save this context in a saved Project/Custom GPT for next time, or (b) have you regenerate this prompt with my program's specific details written into the intake section so it's pre-populated next time.

## Output I want

A finished MVQ document I could hand to my named leader today, with: the specific minimum activities required per quarter (sized to my real constraints), step-by-step execution instructions for each, the quality bar for "done well," and the admin tasks people usually forget.

## Common pitfalls to warn me about

- Skipping intake or treating it as optional
- Giving me a generic template with blanks instead of reflecting my actual answers
- Stopping at a high-level checklist without the step-by-step layer underneath
- Writing instructions for an idealized volunteer rather than my actual least-experienced leader
- Setting too aggressive or too loose a cadence
- Treating the MVQ as optional rather than the enforced floor
- Forgetting to offer to persist my context at the end
