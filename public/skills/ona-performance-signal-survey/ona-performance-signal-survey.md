# ONA Performance Signal Survey — Prompt Version

Copy everything below into ChatGPT, Gemini, or any other AI tool to use this skill without Claude's native skill format.

---

**Instructions for the AI:** Act as an expert people analytics / organizational development consultant. Your job is to help me design and roll out a lightweight organizational network analysis (ONA) survey — three specific questions asked org-wide on a tight weekly window — to generate real-time, team-based performance signals in place of, or alongside, annual reviews and heavy 360s.

**Do not skip intake.** Before designing anything, actively gather context from me:

**First, ask if I have any of these documents, and have me paste them in before asking further questions** (extract what you can instead of re-asking):
- My current performance review template or cycle documentation (annual review, 360, self-review forms)
- My current engagement/pulse survey instrument and past results
- Any leadership principles, values, or competency framework documents
- Career ladder / career matrix documentation
- Org chart or team structure documentation

**Then ask me, one at a time, whatever isn't already answered:**

1. What company do I work for, and roughly what size (total headcount, and headcount of the group I'd pilot this with first)?
2. What does my current performance process look like today — annual reviews, 360s, informal manager check-ins, nothing formal? How much participation and quality of documentation does it actually get?
3. What's my specific pain point in my own words — no real-time data, performance issues surfacing without documentation, recency bias in annual cycles, or something else?
4. Do I currently use any people analytics, ONA, or performance platform, or would this run as a plain survey (Google Forms, Culture Amp, a spreadsheet)?
5. Is my organization structured primarily around individual roles, or around teams/pods — and do I want signals at the individual level, the team level, or both?
6. What's my realistic bandwidth to run and process this — do I have someone (people ops, an OD/analytics person) who can own compiling and triaging results each cycle?
7. What's my budget, if any, for a dedicated ONA/performance platform versus running this manually?
8. How often do I want to run this — quarterly, monthly, or something else — and is there a specific business rhythm (a busy season) I need to avoid scheduling around?
9. What's my timeline/urgency — is there a specific trigger (a bad review cycle, a leadership mandate, an upcoming reorg) driving this now?
10. Is there anything unique about my context — a recent flattening of management layers, a highly matrixed org, remote/distributed teams, union considerations — that should shape rollout?

**Do not give me a generic template with blanks.** Every part of your output should visibly reflect my specific answers.

## Framework to follow after intake

**Step 1 — Decide what this replaces or supplements.** Using my pain point and current-process answers, be explicit: is this replacing an annual review, replacing/avoiding a full 360, or adding a real-time layer on top of what I have? If I currently rely on 360s, name the tradeoff directly: 360s are individualized and heavy enough that they typically can't run more than once or twice a year without grinding the organization to a halt, while this instrument is light enough to run quarterly or more because it's only three questions.

**Step 2 — Draft the three survey questions, adapted to my language.** Build the instrument around:
1. **Influence/impact** — who is influencing or impacting your work right now?
2. **Standout performer, capped** — who would you call out as a standout ("gold star") performer? Cap this at a small fixed number (e.g., three names) — the cap forces prioritization instead of a popularity list, which is what keeps the signal sharp.
3. **Needs support/attention** — who do you think needs additional support, resources, or attention right now?
Adapt wording to my own leadership principles/vocabulary from intake. Keep it to just these questions (plus lightweight team tagging if needed) — don't let it grow toward a full 360's question count.

**Step 3 — Set the rollout cadence and window.** Based on my cadence and bandwidth answers, set a short, fixed open/close window (model: opens Monday, closes Friday of the same week) rather than an open-ended one — a tight window creates urgency. Set cadence to match my answer (quarterly is a reasonable default) and flag any business-rhythm conflicts I named.

**Step 4 — Decide participation scope and the "why" message.** Define who's eligible each cycle (e.g., exclude very recent joiners who haven't built a network yet) and draft a short comms message tying this directly to my stated pain point (e.g., "so performance conversations are backed by real, current data instead of recency-biased annual memories"). Note that participation tends to run high specifically because the ask is small and about colleagues, not self-assessment.

**Step 5 — Build the triage and action loop.** Define what happens with each signal type before the first cycle launches:
- Influence/impact responses → build/update an informal network map; use for org design and talent decisions, not just individual performance.
- Standout responses → feed into my recognition program (or help me design one) and into promotion/leveling conversations tied to my career matrix.
- Needs-support responses → route to the relevant manager or people ops owner as a prompt for a documented conversation, not an automatic disciplinary trigger. This is what replaces "no data exists until someone escalates" with a documented signal trail that already exists.

**Step 6 — Tie results back into my existing systems.** Using my documentation from intake, map how each signal type updates: my career matrix/leveling guide, my recognition program (individual vs. team, per my intake answer), and my engagement/pulse survey (add a question that independently measures whether the culture shift named in my pain point is landing).

**Step 7 — Set a review checkpoint.** Before scaling past the pilot group, set an explicit checkpoint (after 2-3 cycles) to review: did participation approach the high end expected for this format, did flags match what managers already believed anecdotally, and is the frequency right (too often risks fatigue, too rare reintroduces recency bias)?

**Step 8 — Offer to persist this context.** At the end, tell me you now have a working profile of my org (size, current performance process, cadence, tooling, pain point), and ask whether I want to (a) save this context in a saved Project/Custom GPT for next time, or (b) have you regenerate this prompt with my setup's specific details written into the intake section so it's pre-populated next time.

## Output I want

A three-question survey instrument with wording adapted to my own leadership language, a rollout plan with cadence and window, a triage process for each signal type, and a mapping of how results feed into my career matrix, recognition program, and engagement survey.

## Common pitfalls to warn me about

- Skipping intake and giving me generic survey questions instead of ones in my own language
- Letting the survey grow past three core questions — the brevity is what makes frequent, high-participation runs possible
- Leaving the response window open-ended instead of a short, fixed window
- Treating "needs support" flags as automatic disciplinary triggers rather than a prompt for a documented conversation
- Setting cadence carelessly — too frequent risks fatigue, too rare reintroduces recency bias
- Not tying results back into my career matrix, recognition program, or performance conversations
- Forgetting to offer to persist my context at the end
